# Hi, I'm Brett

I'm interested in how complex systems observe the world, make decisions, and act effectively within real-world constraints.

My interest in systems began long before software. As an undergraduate, I was drawn to politics and law because they are among the most influential systems humans have created. Over time, however, I became increasingly interested in a different class of systems: those that can be designed, analyzed, tested, and improved through engineering.

That curiosity eventually led me into software, data, automation, and artificial intelligence.

Today, this GitHub serves as both a professional portfolio and an ongoing record of that exploration.

I learn best by building, which is why most of the ideas that interest me eventually become projects.

---

## The Questions That Keep My Attention

Over time, I became fascinated by systems that govern outcomes, moved from studying social systems to engineering systems, and now explore how increasingly capable decision-making systems can be made reliable, understandable, and useful.

Most of the work you'll find here is connected by a common set of questions:

- How do complex systems make reliable decisions under uncertainty?
- What separates trustworthy automation from fragile automation?
- How can intelligent systems remain understandable and accountable as they become more capable?
- What makes a system resilient when the real world rarely behaves as expected?
- How should humans and intelligent machines collaborate?
- How can technology expand human capability rather than simply replace effort?

The technologies, tools, and implementation details evolve over time. The underlying questions remain surprisingly consistent.

## Areas I'm Exploring

I tend to be less interested in individual technologies than in the systems they make possible.

When approaching a new problem, my instinct is usually to understand the rules, constraints, incentives, and interactions that shape the behavior of the system as a whole. That perspective has followed me from the study of social systems into software systems, and increasingly into intelligent and autonomous systems.

My interests currently span several overlapping domains where systems are designed, operated, measured, and improved:

- Software architecture and engineering
- Data-driven decision systems
- Workflow orchestration and automation
- Operational reliability and resilience
- ML Ops and production machine learning
- Agentic and autonomous systems
- Observability and system transparency

Increasingly, I'm interested in what happens when these disciplines move beyond purely digital environments and begin interacting with the physical world.

That has led me to explore topics such as robotics, autonomous systems, intelligent sensing, embedded computing, and the broader challenge of building systems that can safely translate information into action.

---

## Looking Forward

One of the ideas that increasingly motivates my work is the possibility of using engineering, automation, and intelligence to create entirely new capabilities for people.

Whether the domain is software, industry, agriculture, infrastructure, or something else entirely, I am interested in systems that allow people to accomplish more than would otherwise be possible.

Many of the future projects that appear here will likely be experiments in that direction.

Not simply building technology for its own sake, but exploring how thoughtfully designed systems can improve the way people work, make decisions, and interact with the world around them.

---

## Engineering Principles

A few ideas repeatedly shape how I think about systems:

- Reliability before sophistication.
- Clear behavior beats clever implementation.
- Observability is a feature.
- Modularity enables evolution.
- Automation should increase understanding, not reduce it.
- Intelligent systems should remain accountable to the people who use them.
- Every abstraction hides a tradeoff.

Every repository here represents an attempt to better understand a problem, a technology, or a system that I find interesting.

Some explore software systems.
